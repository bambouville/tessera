tessera — icloud sync

P8 layer 3 — the piece layers 1+2 deliberately punted. Host configs, jump chains, identities, port forwards, and trust hints stay in step through the user's own iCloud; appearance & settings move once at restore. Never passwords, private keys, or trust pins — the same allowlist the nearby bootstrap already moves, over a third transport. Companion to sync.html (S1–S10 contract); nothing here reopens it.

◆one envelope, three transports
The bootstrap manifest's typed allowlist is the wire format. Nearby channel, Handoff descriptor, and iCloud records all speak it — same UUIDs, same classification table, same CI gate.
◆steady state, not ceremony
iCloud keeps hosts in step in the background. The ceremonies — code compare, Face ID grants — stay nearby, where a live peer actually exists.
◆config only
The map syncs; the keys to it never do. A restored host still asks for a credential once, exactly like a bootstrapped one.
◆explainable rules
Last edit wins, whole-record, everywhere. Delete means delete — with a 30-day undo behind it. Two sentences, no sync dashboard, no conflict queue.
◆hints inform, never decide
Host-key fingerprints — public data by nature — travel as syncable hints so informed TOFU survives a cloud restore. Every trust pin is still born of an explicit per-device confirm.

I1Opting in — once, honestly

Existing installs meet the choice card exactly once — on the hosts page at first foreground after the update, never over a live session (fresh installs meet the same choice on the welcome screen, I3). The pitch names both jobs: in step across devices and recoverable if a device is lost. The safe answer is the filled default. After "use my icloud" there is no wizard: the local set publishes, the status row tells the truth, and this device is a peer.

I3Fresh install — restore & the credential bridge

The S1 welcome screen's grayed row goes live. It is enabled only when there's something to restore — an iCloud account on the device and a zone with records — and its subtitle says what's waiting before anyone commits. The flow mirrors the nearby bootstrap receipt with the honest deltas: the account is the binding (no code, no Face ID), hints arrive instead of pins — and then it faces the one thing sync can never deliver: credentials. The restore doesn't end at the receipt; it ends at an explicit access step that names the three honest ways to connect — a nearby key grant, a manual key install, or one password per host. Shown on a new iPad; the two densest cards are drawn in compact idiom at the end of the section.

the credential bridge — said out loud, once. This card is the explicit statement the restore owes the user: your hosts are here; your passwords and private keys are not, and never will be — here's exactly how each gap closes. Three lanes cover everyone: peer at hand (the fast lane, next frame), self-serve (the existing keys → public-key flow), or lazy (the S6 card at first connect). I4Steady state — it just stays in step

Tuesday: a host added on the phone. On a good day it's on the iPad in seconds; in reality arrival is at next foreground — push is best-effort, and a terminated app or Low Power Mode means minutes to hours. Edits and deletes behave identically: they arrive. The only standing surface is the settings status row — and it has more to say than three states (below).

I5Same host twice — one rule, then a receipt

Before sync, both devices independently added zeus — same endpoint, different UUIDs, drifted details. At first join the records collide by endpoint (the typed transport·user@address:port·via-chain coordinates the continuity resolver already matches on) and merge automatically — no prompt, because the join card (I2) already announced the count, and three colliding hosts as three modals is how "the one and only sync card" dies. The rule is the same one as everywhere else: the copy edited most recently wins, whole-record (most recently = server receipt order — device wall clocks are never trusted across devices, I8); tags and port forwards union in; the duplicate retires with a tombstone. What one of those merges did lives where it can actually be checked: the host's detail page.

I6Trust & credentials after a cloud restore

A restored device's first connect to a host runs the S5 card — but the hint arrived over iCloud, and the card grades it: a hint is labeled by the strength of the channel that carried it, not rendered as one uniform green. Neutral recall for cloud-delivered ("your iPad recorded this"); green stays reserved for verified channels (a SAS-bound bootstrap hint, or a match against an existing local pin). The pin this tap creates is still born on this device, and the credential step right after is still the S6 card — now with a memory of how many passwords you've typed. The same tiered line rides the changed-key card: a rotation another device already accepted arrives with context instead of bare alarm (V3). iCloud changes how the map arrives, never what trust costs.

I8Mechanisms — what's actually in the cloud

The sync payload is the bootstrap manifest's typed allowlist, one record per thing, in a custom zone of the user's private iCloud database. The manifest adapter is the single serializer — the nearby channel, Handoff descriptors, and these records are three transports over one wire format, which is why a bootstrapped device joining iCloud merges with zero duplicates: the UUIDs already match.

Transport honesty, said plainly: every content field rides in CloudKit's encrypted record values — sealed on-device, keys chained through your icloud keychain, which Apple cannot read. That is end-to-end for every user with icloud keychain on (Advanced Data Protection not required; ADP users were already end-to-end — an earlier draft of this section called the transport "not end-to-end, a deliberate choice," on a premise that stopped being true: per-field E2E has shipped since iOS 15). What stays standard-iCloud: record shape and timing — a zone of UUID-named records with modification times, never contents. Two honest costs, both designed for: encrypted fields can't be server-queried (irrelevant — the zone is fetched whole, never queried), and icloud keychain must stay on (its own explained states, I3/I4). Adopting this before the zone ships is a field-type choice; after, it's a re-upload of every record plus version-gating — the cheapest this decision will ever be, so an engineering spike gates the plumbing milestone. And the bright line holds: credentials never enter the zone at all. E2E is not an argument to sync keys — the credential bridge exists because access is earned per device (I3). No Tessera account, no vendor server, no analytics on sync contents. Size is a non-issue — ~1 KB per host against the user's own quota; the manifest's existing caps (≤1000 hosts, 1 MiB) bound the zone.

Version skew, the write side: the sidecar preserves fields it doesn't know opaquely, per record, and re-emits them untouched on publish — so an older build's edit can never roll back a newer build's data (under whole-record last-edit-wins, a naive old-build write is exactly that rollback). This is a deliberate, recorded departure from the manifest's reject-unknown decoder posture: a poisoned nearby manifest is trust-relevant — it could pre-seed false fingerprints — while your own newer build's fields are not. What an old build can't render still degrades to no-update, and the status row says so once (I4).

Ordering authority: "edited most recently" is decided by CloudKit server receipt order (record change tags), never by device wall clocks — a phone that's wrong by minutes must not silently win or lose edits. The dates printed on merge receipts (I5) are for humans; they never order anything.

Why not SwiftData↔CloudKit mirroring: mirroring can't enforce @Attribute(.unique) on PersistedHost.id, forces every relationship optional, and demands a custom migration stage per schema change — for a sync that is, at heart, "apply this allowlist to the store." So sync lives in a sidecar store: records map through the manifest adapter into the existing SwiftData models on arrival, and local edits publish out of the same adapter. PersistedHost / Identity schema stays byte-identical (the iOS 26 migration-crash rule is untouched), and the posture tests stay green — cloudKitDatabase: .none on the model container, no kSecAttrSynchronizable anywhere. The CloudKit client is the only new dependency surface.

Lifecycle honesty: sync may write config while the app is locked — it can never act: no arrival opens a connection, grants a key, or pins a host (never-auto-connect holds; the lock still wins). Push failures surface only as the status row (I4), never as alerts. Two devices on different Apple accounts simply never see a zone — the welcome row reports "no icloud account" (I3) and nearby bootstrap is the path, as designed in S1.

The nearby flow doubles as the grant flow — no fork. Bootstrap's manifest import is idempotent (adopt-UUID, skip-on-collision), so "send/receive setup" rerun after an iCloud restore is a data no-op whose real payload is the per-host key authorization (I3): the S1 surfaces are reused verbatim, with one added copy line ("setup already in sync via icloud — this transfer grants keys only") on the origin's grant card. No grant-only mode, no new protocol, and the SAS + Face ID order is inherited unchanged. The device key stays lazy in the cloud era too: created on the first grant or credential tap, never at restore.

I9One mental model — four mechanisms, four jobs

iCloud sync doesn't replace anything layers 1+2 built; it takes over the one job they were never meant to do — keeping topology in step when the devices aren't in the same room. Each mechanism answers a different "when," and each trusts a different anchor:

Composition rules (how the nearby era and the cloud era fit):
1 · Bootstrap, then turn on iCloud → zero duplicates. Same UUIDs, same envelope — the merge in I5 finds nothing to do. The two paths were built on one wire format precisely so this upgrade is free.
2 · Restore from iCloud, then bridge the credential gap (I3). The restore ends in the access card: grant keys nearby (the S1 flow rerun — idempotent import makes the data a no-op, the grants are the payload), install the key yourself, or pay one password per host at first connect (S6, with S7 opportunistic when devices meet). Two halves of one onboarding: iCloud moves the map asynchronously; the nearby ceremony hands out keys when a live, authenticated peer exists.
3 · Look & settings move once — at restore into an empty install — or anytime, deliberately, via nearby "send setup." Steady-state iCloud sync never touches them, and a join into a configured device leaves its theme alone.
4 · Nothing in iCloud can open a connection or grant access. Sync writes config; acting on it always takes a tap, a credential, or a Face ID.

secrets never move — here either
Passwords and private keys stay in per-device keychain & Secure Enclave. The allowlist is enforced by CI, not by carefulness.
configuration, never access
iCloud restores the map, not the keys to it. A restored device still earns every connection — a credential or a nearby Face ID grant.
pins never sync — and hints are graded
Fingerprints travel as syncable hints, labeled by channel strength: green for verified (SAS-bound), neutral recall for cloud-delivered. Every pin is an explicit, per-device decision.
no grants through the cloud
There is no live authenticated channel in a database. Key installs stay a Face ID ceremony between devices that are actually present.
off by default
One toggle; the safe answer is the filled default on the choice card; the delete rule is stated before opt-in, not discovered after.
no silent reshaping
The first join announces its merges once; the rules never change after — last edit wins everywhere, delete means delete (30-day undo), stale devices re-join, a removed zone is never republished.
your container, unreadable even there
Private iCloud database; every content field end-to-end encrypted — keys live in your iCloud Keychain. Record shape and timing stay standard iCloud, and the mock says so. No vendor servers, no Tessera account.
no device enumeration
We never list your devices — CloudKit can't disclose a list, and we wouldn't build one. A record names only the device that wrote it.
the lock still wins
Sync may write config while locked; it can never act. Every connection still starts with a tap.
